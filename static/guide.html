How to Get an Internship: A Practical Guide

Most students lose the internship race before they even apply - by searching in the wrong month. Here's when hiring actually happens by industry, where to look, and how to stand out once you do.

When internships actually open, by industry

This is the part most students get wrong: application windows don't start in spring before the summer you're applying for. For competitive industries, they start more than a year ahead.

If a window for your target industry has already closed, don't panic - startups, government, and nonprofits hire later and often on a rolling basis, and plenty of companies run off-cycle spring or fall programs too.

Where to actually look

  • Your school's career platform (Handshake, or whatever your university uses) - often has postings that never make it to public job boards.
  • Company career pages directly - the first place a role is posted, before it's picked up by LinkedIn, Indeed, or other aggregators. This is also the only place to catch roles that never get syndicated at all.
  • LinkedIn and general job boards - wide reach, but by the time a posting shows up here it's often already drawing hundreds of applicants.
  • Career fairs and info sessions - also a chance to get a name and email for a direct follow-up instead of applying cold.

The catch with company career pages: checking them yourself means refreshing hundreds of individual sites by hand, which nobody actually has time to do.

How many internships to apply to

Treat it like a portfolio, not a single bet:

  • Competitive fields (finance, big tech, consulting): 15-25 applications.
  • Less competitive fields: 8-12 applications is often enough.
  • Split your list: a handful of reach companies, a larger group of realistic targets, and a few safety options you'd genuinely be glad to get.

Tailor each application to the role rather than sending an identical resume everywhere - even small changes (relevant coursework, matching keywords from the posting) make a measurable difference in who gets a callback.

Networking still matters more than most students think

  • Request informational interviews with people who've interned where you want to - classmates, alumni, or people you meet at career fairs are all fair game to ask.
  • Send a short, specific introduction email to a company you're interested in even if they have no posted opening. Plenty of internships get created for the right person before a listing ever goes up.
  • Tell people what you're looking for. Family, friends, professors, and classmates can't refer you to something they don't know you want.

How to spot a fake posting

Internship scams target students specifically, and they're common enough to watch for:

  • No verifiable company presence online, or a residential address instead of a real office.
  • Any request for payment - for "training," "equipment," or "processing" - before you've been hired.
  • A request for your Social Security number or bank account details before you've received a written offer and are going through official onboarding with an HR representative.

When in doubt, your school's career center can usually confirm whether a posting or company is legitimate.

Before you accept an offer

  • Get the offer in writing - title, location, and pay or benefits - before you accept anything verbally.
  • If you're waiting on a preferred employer, it's normal to ask the offering company for 2-3 weeks to decide.
  • Have a legal services office (most schools have one, free for students) review any contract, NDA, or non-compete before you sign.
  • If you accept, decline other offers promptly and professionally - reneging on an accepted offer can follow you and affects other students from your school too.

Frequently asked questions

When do summer internship applications open?

It depends heavily on industry. Investment banking and consulting (MBB) often open as early as December-January, over a year before the internship starts. Big tech usually opens July-October. Startups, government, and nonprofits mostly hire on rolling or later timelines, from January through spring.

How many internships should I apply to?

For competitive fields like finance, tech, and consulting, aim for 15-25 applications. For less competitive fields, 8-12 is often enough. Split your list into a few reach companies, a larger group of realistic targets, and a few safety options.

How do I know if an internship posting is a scam?

Red flags include a residential address instead of a real office, upfront fees for "training" or "equipment," requests for your Social Security number or bank details before you've been officially hired, and no verifiable company presence online. Legitimate employers never ask for payment to work for them.
